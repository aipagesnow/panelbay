Postcrete bags per post

The bags follow the post and the hole.

The holes this tally uses

A 100 × 100 mm post, in a hole 300 mm across and 600 mm deep, takes 2 bags of 20 kg. That is the Heidelberg PostFix figure for June 2025.

A 75 × 75 mm post, in a hole 250 mm across and 600 mm deep, is counted at 1½ bags. That is the row in Blue Circle's Postcrete table, December 2023. The tally multiplies it out and rounds up, because bags are sold whole.

A wider hole, soft ground, or a heavy gate needs more than this. Read the bag before you load the car.

Postcrete or mixed concrete?

Postcrete goes in dry. There is no mixing, it sets in minutes, and you can hang panels the same day. That is why most people use it for a garden fence. The bags cost more for the amount of concrete you get.

A mix of cement and ballast is cheaper by volume. It takes longer to set and you have to mix it, but you can make the footing as big as you like. It is the better choice for heavy gates, tall posts in exposed spots, or soft ground that needs a wider hole than the Postcrete table covers.

How to set a post with Postcrete

  1. Dig the hole three times the post width and a third of the fence height deep.
  2. Stand the post in the hole and fill it a third of the way with water.
  3. Pour the Postcrete in evenly around the post until no standing water shows.
  4. Sprinkle water on top, then level the post before it sets.
  5. Once set, cover the top with soil or turf.

Questions

How many bags of Postcrete per fence post?

For a 100 × 100 mm post in a hole 300 mm wide and 600 mm deep, 2 bags of 20 kg. For a 75 × 75 mm post in a hole 250 mm wide and 600 mm deep, 1½ bags. Both figures come from the Blue Circle table, and the hole width changes the count more than anything else.

How big should the hole be?

Blue Circle says to dig three times wider than the post and as deep as a third of the fence height above the ground. For a 1.8 m fence on 100 mm posts, that is about 300 mm across and 600 mm deep.

How long does Postcrete take to set?

Blue Circle gives 5 to 10 minutes to set, and says you can fix the panel once that first 10 minutes has passed. Cold weather slows it down.

Can I use Postcrete for a gate post?

Blue Circle lists small and medium gateposts, for gates under 1.0 m wide. For a wider or heavier gate, use a mixed concrete footing or follow the gate maker's instructions.

Is Postcrete the same as PostFix or Postmix?

They do the same job, but each maker publishes its own table. Heidelberg PostFix gives the same 2 bags for a 100 mm post in a 300 × 600 mm hole. Read the table on the bag you buy.

Do I mix Postcrete with water first?

No. Fill the hole a third of the way with water, then pour the dry Postcrete in around the post until the water is covered. Sprinkle a little water on top and level the post.
