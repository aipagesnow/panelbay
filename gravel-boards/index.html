Gravel boards: how many, and which kind

The board under the panel, so the panel stays off the soil.

What the board is doing

A gravel board is the horizontal timber under the panel. It keeps the panel off the soil. A usual board is about 150 mm deep and the same width as the bay. Saw the cut board to the cut length.

Fitting them

With concrete posts, drop the gravel board into the post slots first, then slide the panel in on top. With timber posts, fix the board across the bottom of the bay before hanging the panel, and set it level even if the ground is not. On a slope, step the boards with the panels.

Using a 300 mm board, or stacking two 150 mm boards, raises the fence. Add the extra height to the post length. The posts page has the lengths.

Questions

How many gravel boards do I need?

One per bay, so the same number as panels. A 10 m run of 1.83 m panels needs 6, with the last one cut to match the cut panel. The calculator above counts them for your run.

Concrete or timber gravel boards?

Concrete if you have concrete posts, or if the ground stays wet, because it does not rot. Timber if you have timber posts and want something light and easy to cut and screw. Timber boards will need replacing sooner.

What size are gravel boards?

Most are 1830 mm long to match a standard panel. Concrete boards are usually 150 mm or 300 mm high and 50 mm thick. Timber boards are usually 150 mm high.

How heavy is a concrete gravel board?

A smooth 1830 × 150 × 50 mm concrete board weighs about 30 kg. Two people should carry and place it.

Can you cut a concrete gravel board?

Yes, with a disc cutter or an angle grinder and a stone blade, wearing eye, ear and dust protection. The steel rods inside mean it is slower than cutting timber.

Should the gravel board touch the ground?

It can sit on or just in the soil, which is its job. The panel should not. Keep the bottom of the panel clear of the soil and any gravel.
