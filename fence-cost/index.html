Where the money goes

On a panel fence, the panel and the labour make up about two thirds of the bill. A typical fitted bay of one panel and one post breaks down roughly like this, from the Best Builders 2026 guide:

What pushes the price up

  • Height. Taller fences need longer posts and deeper holes.
  • Concrete posts and gravel boards, which cost more but do not rot.
  • Slopes, roots, rock and old footings, which slow the digging.
  • Access. If everything has to be carried through the house, expect to pay for it.
  • Short runs, because most fitters have a minimum charge.

Questions

How much does a new fence cost per metre in the UK?

For a fence 1.8 m high, supplied and fitted, plan on about £60 to £100 a metre for lap panels, £90 to £130 for closeboard and £130 to £200 for composite. Those ranges come from published 2026 UK cost guides, listed under sources.

How much is a 6 ft fence panel fitted?

About £100 to £180 a panel, including the post, gravel board, Postcrete and labour. A heavy closeboard panel on concrete posts sits at the top of that range.

How much is labour for fencing?

Labour is usually around half the bill. Published rates run from about £25 to £45 a metre, £30 to £60 a panel, or £200 to £400 a day for a fitter. Slopes, hard ground and poor access cost more.

How much does it cost to remove an old fence?

Usually £10 to £25 a metre, depending on what is coming out and whether a skip can get close. Old concrete footings take longer.

Is it cheaper to put up a fence yourself?

The materials are about half the fitted price. For closeboard, plan on £35 to £60 a metre in materials. You save the labour but you need the time, a post hole digger, and a helper for the posts.

Do concrete posts cost more?

Yes, they add roughly £12 to £20 a post over timber. They do not rot, so they often pay for themselves when the panels are replaced and the posts stay.
