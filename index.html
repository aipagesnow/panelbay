Fence panel calculator

Measure the run in metres. The tally is the panels, posts, Postcrete and gravel boards for that one line.

How this tally counts

A bay is one panel width. The box starts at 1.83 m, the usual UK panel. Change it if yours are a different width.

Full bays are whole panels. If the last bay comes up short, the tally calls it a cut panel. You still buy a whole panel and saw it down. A remainder of 20 mm or less is taken up in the joints, and it is not a cut. If the tape is up to 20 mm short of another full panel, that panel is counted and there is nothing to cut. Either way, the tally shows the millimetres.

A gate is taken out of the run before the panels are counted. It still counts as a bay for the posts. It does not get a gravel board.

Posts are one more than the bays, unless an end fixes to a wall. Postcrete follows the post size and the hole on the Postcrete page, in 20 kg bags.

A worked example: a 10 m run

Take a straight 10 m run with standard 1.83 m panels, posts at both ends and no gate. 10 ÷ 1.83 is 5 full panels, which use 9.15 m. That leaves 0.85 m, so a sixth panel is bought whole and cut to 850 mm.

Six panels make six bays, and six bays need seven posts. With 100 × 100 mm posts in 300 × 600 mm holes, that is 2 bags of Postcrete a post, so 14 bags. Each panel sits on its own gravel board, so 6 boards, with the last one cut to match the cut panel.

Questions

How many fence panels do I need?

Divide the run by the panel width and round up. A 10 m run of 1.83 m panels is 5 full panels and a sixth cut to 0.85 m, so you buy 6. If you have a gate, take its width off the run first.

How many posts do I need for my panels?

One more post than bays, so 6 panels need 7 posts. Take one off for each end that fixes to a wall instead of a post. A gate counts as a bay, so it adds one post, not two.

Is 1.83 m the standard UK fence panel width?

Yes. Most UK panels are 1.83 m (6 ft) wide and come in heights from about 0.9 m to 1.83 m. Some ranges are narrower, so check the panel you are buying and change the width box if it differs.

Does the tally allow for the width of the posts?

No. It counts panels at the panel width only. Each post takes up some of the run, so on a long run the last cut panel will often come out a little shorter than the tally shows. Leaving the posts out means the tally never leaves you a panel short.

How much Postcrete per fence post?

Blue Circle's table gives 2 bags of 20 kg for a 100 × 100 mm post in a hole 300 mm wide and 600 mm deep, and 1½ bags for a 75 × 75 mm post in a hole 250 mm wide. The Postcrete page has the full table.

What about a sloping garden?

Count the run along the level, not up the slope. On a slope the panels are usually stepped, so the posts need to be longer on the downhill side. This tally does not work out the steps.
