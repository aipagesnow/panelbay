Fence posts: how many, how far apart, how deep

The tally gives the number of posts. It does not choose their length.

Corner and post length

This tally is one straight run. For an L shape, count each side on its own. Give the corner post to one side only, or the two tallies will both claim it.

A panel 1.8 m high is often set on a 2.4 m post, with about 600 mm in the ground. A heavy gate or soft ground may want more in the hole. Check that against the post you buy.

Post length and depth by fence height

Bury about a quarter of the post. Pick the post length from the height of the fence, not the panel.

75 mm or 100 mm posts

For a fence 1.8 m high, go for 100 × 100 mm. It takes the wind load better and gives screws more to bite. Use 75 × 75 mm for fences up to about 1.2 m, or where the garden is sheltered. Gate posts want 100 mm at least, and a heavy gate wants more.

Spacing

With panels, the spacing is set by the panel: one panel between each pair of posts. With closeboard you choose it, up to 3 m between centres. The closeboard calculator works that out.

Concrete fence posts

Slotted concrete posts are the most common choice for panel fencing. They are steel reinforced and have slots cast down their length. The concrete gravel board and the panel slide into those slots, so nothing is screwed or nailed to the post.

Which post goes where

A slotted fence uses four shapes of post. The slots decide where each one goes, so check every corner and end before you order.

A straight run with an end post at each end needs one more post than panels. Ten panels take nine intermediate posts and two end posts. Count corners, 3-way posts and gates on their own.

Closeboard can go on concrete too, using morticed or recessed posts. On a morticed post the rails sit in holes through the post, and the post stays on show. On a recessed post the post is hidden behind the boards. Ibstock lists a 2515 mm recessed post for a closeboard fence 1.65 m high, or 1.8 m high with a 150 mm gravel board.

What length of concrete post

The length on a concrete post is the whole post, including the part that goes in the ground. Add the panel and the gravel board to get the height above ground, then add about 600 mm for the hole.

That is why a 2.4 m post works for some 6 ft fences and not others. With 600 mm buried, it leaves 1.8 m above the ground. That holds a panel 1.65 m high on a 150 mm board, but a panel 1.8 m high on the same board needs 2.55 m in total, so the next size up is 2.7 m.

You will also see 2.36 m (7 ft 9 in) posts listed for a fence 1.8 m high. They leave a little under 600 mm in the ground. Ibstock's Strongcast Professional posts have a dig depth mark on them, which helps to set every post at the same depth.

Other posts of a similar length weigh more. Jewson lists a 2.36 m Supreme Geeco slotted post at 46 kg. MKM lists a 100 × 125 mm wet-cast post of the same length at 51 kg. Ibstock describes its 50 kg corner post as a two-person lift, so have a second person for every post, to carry it and to hold it upright while it is set.

Gravel boards and panels in the slots

Drop the concrete gravel board into the slots first, then slide the panel in on top. Some intermediate posts have a ledge inside the slot, about 600 mm from the bottom, for the board to rest on. The gravel boards page has board sizes and weights.

The panel has to fit the slot, both in width and in thickness. Most panels and concrete boards are 1830 mm (6 ft) wide, and some are 1800 mm, so buy the panels, boards and posts as one set. Ibstock's slots are 55 mm wide and 20 mm deep. Check the thickness of the panel frame against that, especially on decorative panels.

The gap between two posts is narrower than the panel, because each edge sits inside a slot. Set out each bay with a real panel or board rather than with the tape alone. If the panels rattle or lift in the wind, slot clips are made for this, such as Ibstock's Scorpion clip.

Setting concrete posts

Postcrete is made for concrete posts as well as timber. Blue Circle's hole rule is the same for both: three times the width of the post, and a third of the fence height deep.

Blue Circle's bag table stops at 100 mm square posts. In a hole 300 mm wide and 600 mm deep, it gives 2 bags of 20 kg. Most slotted posts are around 80 to 125 mm across, so treat 2 bags per post as the least you will need and buy a spare. The Postcrete page has the full table.

Run a string line along the fence before you dig. Check each post is upright on two faces next to each other, and prop it while the Postcrete sets.

Concrete posts are steel reinforced, and cutting one shorter can expose the steel or remove the concrete that protects it. Work out the length before you order. Drilling one for a bracket or trellis needs a hammer drill and a masonry bit. Timber is easier to cut and fix to, which is why it suits closeboard and anything screwed on.

Repair spurs for a broken timber post

If a timber post has rotted at ground level but is still sound higher up, a concrete repair spur can hold it up without replacing the fence. The spur is a short concrete post, set in the ground beside the timber one and bolted to it.

Supreme Concrete's SPR100 spur is 1000 × 75 × 75 mm and weighs about 13 kg. It has 12 mm holes for the bolts. Ibstock also makes 100 × 100 mm spurs in longer lengths.

  1. Dig a hole about 600 mm (2 ft) deep against the post.
  2. Stand the spur in the hole and mark the bolt holes on the post.
  3. Take the spur out and drill the post level, for a 12 mm bolt.
  4. Bolt the spur to the post and fill the hole with concrete.

If the timber is soft well above the ground, a spur has nothing to hold on to, and the post needs replacing.

Questions

How far apart should fence posts be?

For panels, the posts sit one panel apart, so with 1.83 m panels the centres are about 1.83 m plus the post width. Set out the posts as you hang each panel rather than marking every hole first. For closeboard, posts go at 3 m centres at most.

How deep should a fence post be?

As a rule, a quarter of the post's length goes in the ground, or a third of the fence height above it. For a fence 1.8 m high that is about 600 mm. Go deeper in soft or exposed ground.

What length of post for a 6 ft fence?

A 2.4 m (8 ft) post. That leaves 1.8 m above the ground and 600 mm below it. If the panel sits on a gravel board, you need a longer post, as the concrete post answer below shows.

Should I use 75 mm or 100 mm posts?

100 × 100 mm posts are stiffer and are the safer choice for fences 1.8 m high, exposed gardens and gate posts. 75 × 75 mm posts are fine for lower fences in sheltered spots. The 100 mm post needs a wider hole and a little more Postcrete.

Timber or concrete posts?

Concrete posts do not rot and suit slotted panels with concrete gravel boards. They are heavy to handle and harder to cut. Treated timber posts are lighter and easier to fix to, and they suit closeboard and panels screwed on with clips.

How many posts for an L-shaped fence?

Count each side as its own run, then take one post off the total because the two sides share the corner post.

What size concrete post for a 6 ft fence?

It depends on the gravel board. With 600 mm in the ground, a 2.4 m (8 ft) post leaves 1.8 m above it, which suits a panel 1.65 m high on a 150 mm board. A panel 1.8 m high on a 150 mm board needs about 2.55 m in total, so buy a 2.7 m (9 ft) post.

How heavy is a concrete fence post?

An 8 ft post weighs roughly 30 to 50 kg. Ibstock's lighter range runs from 33 kg for an intermediate post to 50 kg for a corner post. Other 2.36 m posts are listed at 46 kg and 51 kg. Plan on two people for each post.

Can you cut a concrete fence post shorter?

It is best not to. The posts are steel reinforced, and cutting can expose the steel or remove the concrete that protects it. Work out the length you need before you order, and ask the maker if a post really has to be altered.
