Fence posts: how many, how far apart, how deep

The tally gives the number of posts. It does not choose their length.

Corner and post length

This tally is one straight run. For an L shape, count each side on its own. Give the corner post to one side only, or the two tallies will both claim it.

A panel 1.8 m high is often set on a 2.4 m post, with about 600 mm in the ground. A heavy gate or soft ground may want more in the hole. Check that against the post you buy.

Post length and depth by fence height

Bury about a quarter of the post. Pick the post length from the height of the fence, not the panel.

75 mm or 100 mm posts

For a fence 1.8 m high, go for 100 × 100 mm. It takes the wind load better and gives screws more to bite. Use 75 × 75 mm for fences up to about 1.2 m, or where the garden is sheltered. Gate posts want 100 mm at least, and a heavy gate wants more.

Spacing

With panels, the spacing is set by the panel: one panel between each pair of posts. With closeboard you choose it, up to 3 m between centres. The closeboard calculator works that out.

Questions

How far apart should fence posts be?

For panels, the posts sit one panel apart, so with 1.83 m panels the centres are about 1.83 m plus the post width. Set out the posts as you hang each panel rather than marking every hole first. For closeboard, posts go at 3 m centres at most.

How deep should a fence post be?

As a rule, a quarter of the post's length goes in the ground, or a third of the fence height above it. For a fence 1.8 m high that is about 600 mm. Go deeper in soft or exposed ground.

What length of post for a 6 ft fence?

A 2.4 m (8 ft) post. That leaves 1.8 m above the ground and 600 mm below it.

Should I use 75 mm or 100 mm posts?

100 × 100 mm posts are stiffer and are the safer choice for fences 1.8 m high, exposed gardens and gate posts. 75 × 75 mm posts are fine for lower fences in sheltered spots. The 100 mm post needs a wider hole and a little more Postcrete.

Timber or concrete posts?

Concrete posts do not rot and suit slotted panels with concrete gravel boards. They are heavy to handle and harder to cut. Treated timber posts are lighter and easier to fix to, and they suit closeboard and panels screwed on with clips.

How many posts for an L-shaped fence?

Count each side as its own run, then take one post off the total because the two sides share the corner post.
